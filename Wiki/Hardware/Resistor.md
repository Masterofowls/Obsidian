# How Resistors Work

## Overview
A resistor is a passive electronic component that **opposes the flow of electric current**. It converts electrical energy into heat.

## What It Does
- **Limits current** — protects components from too much current
- **Divides voltage** — creates voltage drops in a circuit
- **Biases transistors** — sets operating points in amplifier circuits

## How It Works
- Made from materials that resist electron flow (carbon, metal film, wire)
- The resistance is measured in **Ohms (Ω)**
- Follows **Ohm's Law**: `V = I × R`
  - V = Voltage (Volts)
  - I = Current (Amps)
  - R = Resistance (Ohms)

## Types
| Type | Usage |
|------|-------|
| Carbon Film | General purpose, cheap |
| Metal Film | Precision, low noise |
| Wirewound | High power, precise |
| Variable (Potentiometer) | Adjustable resistance |

## Power Rating
- Resistors dissipate heat — if current is too high, they burn out
- Rated in **Watts** (1/4W, 1/2W, 1W, etc.)
