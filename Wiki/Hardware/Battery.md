# How Batteries Work

## Overview
A battery converts **chemical energy** into **electrical energy** through electrochemical reactions.

## Basic Chemistry
- A battery has two **electrodes**: anode (negative) and cathode (positive)
- An **electrolyte** allows ions to flow between them
- Chemical reactions at the electrodes create a flow of electrons → electricity

## Lithium-Ion Battery
- Most common in phones, laptops, EVs
- **Anode**: Graphite (stores lithium ions)
- **Cathode**: Lithium metal oxide (LiCoO₂, LiFePO₄)
- **Electrolyte**: Lithium salt in organic solvent

### Charging
- External voltage pushes lithium ions from cathode → anode
- Electrons flow through the external circuit (charging)

### Discharging
- Lithium ions flow from anode → cathode
- Electrons flow through the external circuit (powering your device)

## Accumulator (Rechargeable Battery)
- Same principle as lithium-ion
- Lead-acid (car batteries), NiMH, Li-ion are all accumulators
- Can be charged/discharged hundreds to thousands of cycles
