# What is Atmospheric Pressure

## Overview
Atmospheric pressure is the **weight of the air** above a point, pushing down due to gravity.

## How It Works
- Air has mass → gravity pulls it down
- At sea level: ~101,325 Pa (1 atm) = 14.7 psi
- Decreases with altitude (less air above)

## How It Affects Liquids and Solids

### Liquids
- Atmospheric pressure pushes on liquid surfaces
- **Barometer**: atmospheric pressure supports a column of mercury
- **Drinking straw**: suction creates lower pressure → atmospheric pressure pushes liquid up

### Solids
- Buildings are affected by pressure differences
- High winds create pressure differences that can lift roofs
- Pressure-sealed containers (vacuum bags) use atmospheric pressure to compress
